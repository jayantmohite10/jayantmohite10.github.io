01 / ABOUT

About My Research

My research lies at the intersection of Geospatial AI, Earth Observation, and environmental intelligence, with a focus on transforming scientific advances into scalable, real-world solutions.

I pioneered the Sky–Earth Convergence approach, integrating satellite, UAV, and ground observations with domain-aware AI to enable intelligent Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) across agriculture, forestry, and biodiversity. My work spans precision agriculture, regenerative farming, soil and forest carbon, deforestation, and ecosystem assessment.

A central theme of my research is advancing scalable, explainable, and autonomous geospatial intelligence to bridge the gap between Earth Observation science and actionable sustainability decisions.

02 / ONGOING RESEARCH AND KEY PROJECTS

Ongoing Research and Key Projects

Five connected research programs spanning agriculture, water, sustainability MRV, forestry and foundational geospatial AI methods.

01

Multi-Sensor Remote Sensing and Geospatial AI for Crop Characterization, Health Assessment and Yield Intelligence

Research focused on developing satellite-based and multimodal geospatial intelligence approaches for crop acreage estimation, crop-type discrimination, growth-stage identification, pest and disease assessment, and yield estimation. Early investigations integrated participatory sensing, satellite observations and weather information for citrus gummosis severity classification (Mohite et al., 2016), followed by hybrid classification–clustering techniques for export/non-export grape mapping and crop health assessment using Sentinel-2 (Mohite et al., 2017). Subsequent studies evaluated multispectral satellite and proximal imaging capabilities for detecting low-severity mite infestation in grapes and thrips infestation in capsicum (Mohite et al., 2018a, 2018b).

Further research explored multi-temporal SAR and optical observations for operational rice acreage mapping (Mohite et al., 2018), wheat area and phenology identification (Mohite et al., 2019a), and deep learning-based discrimination of early and late sowing in soybean and cotton (Mohite et al., 2020). Crop production intelligence was investigated by coupling Sentinel-1 observations with the ORYZA crop growth simulation model for rice yield spatialization (Mohite et al., 2019b) and integrating remote sensing and weather observations for soybean yield prediction (Mohite et al., 2023a). More recently, label-efficient sugarcane area mapping was investigated through a hybrid unsupervised–supervised learning approach (Mohite et al., 2024a), while satellite and meteorological observations were explored for cross-pollination risk assessment in hybrid rice seed production (Mohite et al., 2025a).

Research capabilities: Crop classification and mapping, pest/disease assessment, phenology monitoring, SAR–optical fusion, crop simulation integration, machine learning, deep learning, yield prediction and label-efficient mapping.

02

Integrated Earth Observation, Proximal Sensing and Machine Learning for Soil Moisture, Crop Water Stress and Irrigation Intelligence

Research investigated the integration of satellite observations, proximal sensors, hyperspectral measurements and machine learning for monitoring agricultural water availability and supporting field-level resource management. Initial work developed a near-real-time irrigation scheduling decision-support approach combining proximal sensor networks and human participatory sensing (Mohite et al., 2017). Subsequent research demonstrated the application of multi-temporal Sentinel-1 SAR observations for spatiotemporal surface water mapping and regional drought assessment (Mohite et al., 2019). Additional investigations examined the estimation of bare-surface soil moisture using hyperspectral measurements and simulated Sentinel-2 spectral observations (Mohite et al., 2021a).

To address spatial resolution limitations in satellite-derived hydrological variables, research explored downscaling of SMAP soil moisture using MODIS and SRTM observations (Mohite et al., 2022a), alongside spatial downscaling of vegetation optical depth using complementary satellite-derived information (Mohite et al., 2022b). These investigations established methodological approaches for combining coarse-resolution microwave-derived variables with higher-resolution optical and terrain observations. Complementary research also investigated drone-based hyperspectral sensing for detecting crop water stress in maize (Mohite et al., 2022c), extending the sensing framework toward finer-scale crop physiological assessment.

Research capabilities: Soil moisture estimation and downscaling, SAR-based surface water monitoring, hyperspectral sensing, UAV observations, crop water stress detection and precision irrigation decision support.

03

Satellite-Based Monitoring, Reporting and Verification of Regenerative Agriculture, Soil Carbon and Sustainable Farming Practices

Research investigated Earth Observation-driven methods for monitoring sustainable agricultural practices and quantifying associated environmental indicators, particularly cover crop adoption, soil organic carbon and agricultural carbon stocks. Time-series remote sensing observations were used to investigate cover crop detection as an observable indicator of regenerative agriculture practices (Mohite et al., 2023b). Parallel investigations developed approaches for estimating soil organic carbon concentration and soil bulk density using complementary Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multispectral observations to support agricultural carbon stock assessment (Mohite et al., 2023c).

The soil carbon research subsequently expanded toward comparative assessments of remote sensing-derived and Holos-based soil organic carbon stock estimates (Mohite et al., 2025b), followed by the integration of multisource remote sensing observations and soil attributes through ensemble machine learning for large-scale soil organic carbon estimation (Mohite et al., 2026a). Related environmental monitoring research examined the combined influence of agricultural crop residue burning and COVID-19 lockdown conditions on regional air quality using observational datasets (Mohite et al., 2022d). Collectively, these studies contribute methodological building blocks for scalable, observation-driven agricultural sustainability monitoring and carbon-related MRV applications, although formal verification of carbon credits or project-level emissions reductions is not established by these publications alone.

Research capabilities: Regenerative practice detection, cover crop monitoring, soil organic carbon and bulk density estimation, carbon stock assessment, multisensor data fusion, ensemble learning and sustainability MRV indicators.

04

Spaceborne LiDAR and Multi-Source Earth Observation Fusion for Forest Biomass Estimation and Wildfire Risk Intelligence

Research extended geospatial AI methodologies from agricultural monitoring toward forest ecosystem characterization and environmental risk assessment. Investigations focused on estimating forest aboveground biomass using spaceborne Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) observations over Indian forest landscapes (Mohite et al., 2024b). Related work examined fusion of GEDI-derived structural observations with complementary Earth Observation datasets for forest aboveground biomass estimation (Mohite et al., 2024c). These studies investigated the potential of integrating spaceborne LiDAR measurements and other geospatial predictors to estimate forest structural and biomass attributes beyond individual observation footprints.

Complementary research investigated pre-ignition forest fire risk prediction using multi-temporal vegetation indices and machine learning, with California as a case study (Mohite et al., 2026b). This work explored how vegetation dynamics derived from historical satellite observations could be transformed into predictive indicators of fire susceptibility before ignition. Together, these investigations form a technical foundation for forest carbon monitoring, ecosystem structure assessment and proactive environmental risk intelligence. Broader biodiversity assessment or operational wildfire early-warning performance should be described separately where supported by project evidence.

Research capabilities: GEDI-based forest structure assessment, aboveground biomass estimation, LiDAR–optical data fusion, temporal vegetation analytics, predictive machine learning and pre-ignition wildfire risk assessment.

05

Advanced Geospatial AI, SAR–Optical Data Fusion and Spatial–Temporal Learning for Scalable Earth Observation Analytics

Cross-cutting methodological research focused on addressing critical limitations of conventional Earth Observation analytics, including cloud-induced optical data gaps, heterogeneous sensor resolutions, limited ground reference labels and spatial–temporal variability. Studies investigated the use of Sentinel-1 SAR observations with random forest and support vector regression for estimating cloud-free NDVI as an alternative source of vegetation information when optical observations are unavailable (Mohite et al., 2020). Related investigations examined hybrid unsupervised and supervised classification for sugarcane area mapping without conventional training labels (Mohite et al., 2024a), demonstrating a research direction toward reducing dependence on labeled ground observations.

Additional methodological developments explored synergistic use of SAR and multispectral observations for crop characterization and phenology monitoring (Mohite et al., 2018; Mohite et al., 2019a; Mohite et al., 2020), integration of satellite measurements with process-based crop simulation for yield spatialization (Mohite et al., 2019b), and spatial downscaling of soil moisture and vegetation optical depth using complementary environmental predictors (Mohite et al., 2022a, 2022b). More recent investigations incorporated multisource Earth Observation fusion and ensemble learning for soil organic carbon and forest biomass assessment (Mohite et al., 2024c; Mohite et al., 2026a). Collectively, this methodological research supports the development of transferable geospatial AI components for agricultural, forestry and environmental monitoring applications.

Research capabilities: SAR–optical fusion, spatial downscaling, data-gap reconstruction, label-efficient classification, deep learning, ensemble modeling, multitemporal feature extraction and scalable EO analytics.
